Law of Attraction: A Historical Digest

The phrase Law of Attraction has virtually exploded in the post-Y2K world. Through the influence of media and iconic cultural figures, the concept has become almost common knowledge. However, the history of the actual "Law," as we have come to call it, dates back centuries.

“As above, so below. As within, so without.”

The first reference to this idea can be found on the Emerald Tablet, believed to be the first record of man’s conscious awareness of the power of positive thoughts. In layman’s terms, the most basic explanation is that our thoughts manifest our reality. While the law of attraction connection can also be found in any religion, the most obvious ties are revealed in both Hinduism and Buddhism.

The Eastern Connection: Karma & Vibration

This precept correlates deeply with Hindu and Buddhist beliefs. The word Karma comes from the Sanskrit language, the ceremonial language of the Hindu religion. By definition, the word karma means "actions." Buddha taught that the inequalities in human lives were the result of actions—both past and present. Our actions are the final result of applying the Law of Attraction; whether consciously or unconsciously, they stem from our thoughts and feelings.

Parallel to this is the Law of Vibration. Practitioners believe that everything in existence transmits a frequency. Thoughts are no exception; Buddhists believe the contents of the mind are also vibrations. Positive thoughts vibrate at a higher frequency, and nature—or Dharma—ensures that the frequency you transmit is exactly what is returned to you. Buddha believed that the law of Dharma always prevails; if you generate hateful or angry feelings, they can only be returned to you.

The Dawn of New Thought

The "New Thought" movement emerged in the 19th century, led by founding authors like Prentice Mulford, whose 1889 work Thoughts are Things became a mantra for today's teachers. Later, Robert Collier contributed with The Secret of the Ages (1926) and The Secret Power, emphasizing that desire is the planting of the seed—the first step in using the Law of Attraction.

The specific term was solidified by William Walker Atkinson in 1906 through his works Dynamic Thought and Thought Vibration. Following him, Wallace D. Wattles (1910) and Charles Haanel (1912) provided the structural blueprints for modern success. Haanel’s Master Key System was so profound it famously influenced Napoleon Hill, whose Think and Grow Rich (1928) remains an iconic pillar of the movement today.
