Law of Attraction Money & Abundance 101

Your inner state sets the tone for what you create, what you notice, and what you allow into your life.

Most people feel something tighten inside when they think about money. It might be discomfort, doubt, or a quiet sense that money is something other people understand better. If that reaction feels familiar, you are not alone. Most of the world moves through life believing money is scarce or unpredictable, and that belief becomes the first barrier to abundance. Not the economy. Not intelligence. Not luck. The real barrier is the internal relationship you have with money.

Wealth begins with thinking in a certain way.

— Wallace D. Wattles, The Science of Getting Rich

He was not talking about wishful thinking. He meant something far more practical: the way you think about money shapes the way you move toward it. Your thoughts influence your decisions, your habits, your emotional state, and the opportunities you are able to recognize. When you look at people who have built wealth — slowly or suddenly — you will notice something consistent. They think about money without fear. They think about opportunity instead of limitation. They think about value instead of lack. They think from possibility, not toward it. This is the foundation of what many people now call law of attraction money. Your inner state sets the tone for what you create, what you notice, and what you allow into your life.

Changing the Way You See Money

Most of us were raised with ideas that quietly shape our financial identity. "Money doesn't grow on trees." "People like us don't get rich." "Wanting money is greedy." These phrases settle in early and become background noise in adulthood. Without realizing it, people begin to treat money as something distant or morally questionable. Wattles argued that this conditioning keeps people on the competitive plane — a mindset where money is seen as a fixed resource, something you must fight for or take from someone else. But abundance does not grow in that environment. It shrinks.

To shift into the creative plane, you have to change the way you think about money itself. Money is not a personality test. It is not a measure of worth. It is not a moral category. It is a tool. A neutral resource. A form of energy that moves through people, businesses, and ideas. Money is everywhere — in banks, in transactions, in digital systems, in every store you walk past. It flows constantly. It circulates. It moves. And it moves most easily toward people who are open to receiving it and prepared to use it well.

"What you focus on expands."

One of the core principles of the law of attraction is simple: what you focus on expands. If you spend most of your time thinking about debt or financial stress, you reinforce the emotional state connected to those thoughts. You begin to expect more of the same. You move through life bracing for impact instead of opening to possibility. This is not magic. It is psychology. Your mind filters information based on what you believe. If you believe money is scarce, you will notice scarcity. If you believe opportunity is rare, you will overlook the opportunities right in front of you.

Wattles taught that you must hold a clear, steady picture of the life you want and act in alignment with that picture. Not with tension or desperation, but with calm certainty. This is the emotional foundation of law of attraction money: a steady inner state that matches the life you are building.

Value Creates the Bridge to Wealth

Here is the part most people misunderstand. You do not attract money by wanting money. You attract money by becoming valuable. Wattles was ahead of his time here. He taught that wealth comes from creating something that improves life for others. It does not have to be grand or complicated. It simply has to matter. Cutting someone's lawn. Fixing something they cannot fix. Teaching something they do not know. Creating something they need. Sharing knowledge that saves them time, stress, or effort. These are not small things. They are value. And value is the bridge between your inner state and your outer results.

If you want more money, start by asking yourself what you can create, improve, teach, or solve. What can you offer that makes someone's life easier, lighter, or better. This is the practical side of the law of attraction — the part that turns mindset into movement. Money responds to usefulness. It responds to clarity. It responds to people who move through the world with the intention to increase life for others.

Everything you touch, everything you create, and every interaction you have should leave people feeling expanded, not diminished. When you move through life this way, money naturally follows.

— Wallace D. Wattles, The Science of Getting Rich

Money itself has no purpose until it is used. It cannot think, act, or create. It needs a human being to direct it. It needs someone with intention, clarity, and a sense of purpose. When you shift your relationship with money from fear to neutrality, you stop chasing it and start guiding it. You stop seeing it as something outside of you and start seeing it as something that responds to you. This is why Wattles emphasized the impression of increase — the idea that everything you touch, everything you create, and every interaction you have should leave people feeling expanded, not diminished. When you move through life this way, money naturally follows.

The Identity Shift That Changes Everything

To attract abundance, you must change the way you see yourself in relation to money. Not through forced positivity, but through clarity. You are not someone trying to get money. You are someone capable of creating value. You are someone who can grow, learn, and expand. You are someone who can direct money with purpose. This identity shift is the real beginning of abundance.

Changing the way you think and feel about money is the first step. Seeing money as neutral and abundant opens the door. Focusing on possibility instead of limitation changes your emotional baseline. Creating value for others builds the bridge between intention and results. And holding a steady picture of the life you want keeps you aligned with it.

Central Summary

  • You must change the way you think and feel about money
  • Money is not an unattainable resource
  • Money itself is neither good nor bad

  • Identify knowledge or skills you already have that could help someone else
